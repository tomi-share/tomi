# Relay server disk, backup and quotas

Status: **operational note**. No code, spec or test vector is changed.

> This document originally was a long analysis of "several people (several spaces) on one relay". That
> architecture part has been removed: it no longer applies to the initial deployment. Only the points that
> are **still actionable** remain here: what already isolates spaces (for context), privacy with respect to
> the operator, and above all the sizing of **disk + backup + quotas** of the server, which blocks the
> deployment as currently written — **single-space deployments included**.

## Summary

- **The cryptography already isolates spaces.** Each space has its own key, which the relay never holds.
  All signed declarations and the authentication signature include the `space_id`. The relay remains blind
  to content; it only sees opaque bytes and metadata. Nothing to change on the format side.
- **Privacy**: for a person who is not the operator, "your own relay" no longer holds — the operator sees
  their metadata. This must be written down somewhere other people read before they join (§2).
- **Point to watch before deployment**: the disk of the `<relay-host>` server is small (7.9 GB free).
  `infra/backup.sh` no longer duplicates blobs (it keeps `TOMI_BACKUP_KEEP=2` archives by default), but the
  relay total `max_relay_bytes` (1 GiB; no per-space quota by default, configurable from the web panel or
  `max_space_bytes`) and the archives still have to be sized against those 7.9 GB. This must be settled
  before the relay goes into service (§3-4).

## 1. What already isolates spaces (framing reminder)

These protections exist today and do not depend on the relay:

- **Space key** generated by the first device, never sent to the relay (`api-v1.md` §3.2).
  The relay cannot decrypt anything (golden rule 1).
- **All signed declarations bind the `space_id`** in their TBS (`format-v1.md`: `DeviceCert`,
  `MemberList`, `KeyRotation`, `Tombstone`, `HistoryTransfer`, `PinStatement`), as do the key-transport AADs.
  A device checks for `space_mismatch`.
- **The relay checks the `space_id`** of certificates (space creation, device registration) and
  **the authentication signature includes the `space_id`** (`routes/auth.rs`, `verify_relay_auth`).

Consequence: the relay cannot make the content of a space readable. The residual risk lies in the
**metadata** (device names, sizes, timing) and in availability — this is the subject of §2.

## 2. Trust and privacy with respect to the operator

The relay remains blind to content. But for a person who is not the operator:

- **The operator sees the metadata** (`api-v1.md` §1.2): device names, platforms, send and read times,
  sizes, presence. An earlier justification for this exposure, "it is your own relay", no longer holds for
  anyone else.
- **The operator can** revoke a device, erase a space, or stop the service. The operator **cannot** read
  content, nor forge a device that other devices accept.
- **Shared availability**: an outage, an update or a full disk affects everyone.
- Hosting other people's data, even encrypted, makes the operator a data controller for that metadata
  (GDPR). Among close contacts, this is mostly a matter of transparency: tell them what you can see.

Nothing technically prevents this, but these points should be written down somewhere that other people
read before they join.

Two findings outside the quotas, but which **block the deployment as currently written**:

- **nginx already holds ports 80 and 443.** `infra/README.md` and `infra/caddy/Caddyfile` assume
  Caddy as the front end. On this server, one would need either an nginx `server` block forwarding to
  `127.0.0.1:8080` (WebSocket included), or to move the existing sites behind Caddy. The relay listens
  locally, so it is not directly affected.
- **Memory is tight.** The relay uses little (blobs are streamed), but the machine already uses swap.

## 4. Backup and quotas

### 4.1 What consumes disk, besides the data

- **Backups.** `infra/backup.sh` (current version) takes a snapshot of each database with `sqlite3
  .backup` (a consistent hot copy, no `cp` of a `.db`), then `tar`/`gzip` the snapshots **and the
  blobs read in place** (blobs are no longer copied into a working directory). It keeps
  `TOMI_BACKUP_KEEP=2` archives by default (minimum 1) in `/var/backups/tomi`, on the same disk.
  Disk peak ≈ data + `TOMI_BACKUP_KEEP` archives + 1 archive in progress. Encrypted blobs do not
  compress, so each archive is roughly the size of the data. `infra/backup.md` additionally recommends an
  off-machine copy (S3), because a local backup does not protect against losing the disk.
- **History transfer.** At pairing time, the sealed history is stored for up to 7 days
  (`max_transfer_bytes`, 1 GiB by default) and counted in `used_bytes`. Accepting a device can therefore
  double the footprint for a few days, within the quota.
- **What the quota does not count**: the event log, indexes, the SQLite WAL, file metadata.
  A few percent; keep a margin.
- **The relay floor**: `min_free_disk_bytes` (1 GiB) refuses uploads below this threshold (`store.rs`).
  `TOMI_BACKUP_MIN_FREE_BYTES` (2 GB by default) likewise refuses a backup that would drop below the margin.

### 4.2 Budget

Assumptions: 2 GB left to the rest of the machine, 2 GiB preserved for backups
(`TOMI_BACKUP_MIN_FREE_BYTES`), 10 % margin for what the quota does not count. This leaves roughly
**3.5-4 GB** to share between live data and its 2 local archives.

With `KEEP=2`, the local footprint is about: data + 2 archives + 1 archive in progress ≈ **~4 × the
data volume** (blobs dominate and do not compress). With ~4 GB usable, this caps live data at about
**~1 GB** if everything stays local.

| Strategy | Local footprint | Possible live data |
|---|---|---|
| `backup.sh` as is, 2 local archives | ~× 4 | ~1 GB |
| Archive pushed to S3 then deleted (0 local archives kept) | ~× 2 (data + archive in progress) | ~2 GB |
| EBS volume raised to 40 GB | depends on strategy | considerably more |

**Finding, single-space deployments included**: no per-space quota is set by default (production
behaviour); the relay-wide total `max_relay_bytes` (1 GiB by default) is what bounds live data, and a
per-space quota can be set if needed (web panel, `max_space_bytes` or `admin quota`). At 1 GiB of
data, 2 local archives amount to ~4 GB (~× 4), at the limit of the ~3.5-4 GB usable; beyond that, one must
lower `max_relay_bytes`, push the archives off-machine, or enlarge the volume **before going into service**.

Enlarging the EBS volume can be done live (modify the volume, then `growpart` and `resize2fs`). gp3 in
Paris costs on the order of $0.09/GB/month (check the AWS pricing page): +20 GB ≈ $2/month.

### 4.3 Options

- **Backups**: push the archive to S3 and keep no local copy (or just one). This is the most effective lever
  on a small disk, without touching the relay.
- **Quota**: as long as the disk is 20 GB, keep `max_relay_bytes` (and, if needed, a per-space quota) low, or
  enlarge the volume. `min_free_disk_bytes` remains the last safety net on the relay side.
